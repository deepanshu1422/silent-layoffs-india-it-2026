What's actually happening

Staffing firm TeamLease estimates 10,000-15,000 professionals had already exited India's tech industry through "silent layoffs" by May 2026, and projects the full-year total will reach 25,000-35,000. CIEL HR Services, tracking independently, counts roughly 12,000 cuts so far in 2026 and projects 18,000-21,000 by year-end. Combined with 2025's reductions, CIEL HR puts the two-year total as high as 43,000 jobs.

Unlike the layoff waves of 2022-2023, these cuts are not accompanied by press releases, severance headlines, or union notices. They're routed through individual performance reviews and "skill relevance" assessments, one person at a time, which is exactly why the scale of it is so easy to miss until a company's hiring and cutting numbers are placed side by side.

The 5 warning signs

01

Your project gets "paused" without a clear restart date

A real staffing cut, not a real business pause. If a project you're on goes quiet with vague timelines, treat it as a signal, not a coincidence.

02

Performance review language shifts to "skill relevance" or "role redundancy"

This is now the standard phrasing companies use instead of "layoff" in individual exit conversations, per multiple 2026 reports on India's IT restructuring.

03

Lateral hiring freezes in your function while fresher hiring accelerates elsewhere

Tier-1 IT firms cut lateral hiring 0.5-6% in FY26 even while protecting fresher intake, per Xpheno staffing data. Cost structure, not total demand, is driving the shift.

04

You're asked to document your work "for continuity" with no stated reason

A common precursor pattern noted by HR consultants during performance-linked exit cycles: knowledge transfer requests that aren't tied to a visible handover or promotion.

05

Your manager stops including you in forward-looking planning conversations

The most reliable informal signal across every documented case: being quietly excluded from next-quarter or next-project discussions while still being kept on current deliverables.
